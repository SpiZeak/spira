The pot is more than decoration — it directly affects how your plant grows, how often you need to water, and whether the roots stay healthy. Choosing the right pot is one of the most impactful decisions in plant care.

## The only non-negotiable: drainage holes

Every functional plant pot must have at least one drainage hole. Without drainage, excess water has nowhere to go, and roots will eventually rot.

If you fall in love with a beautiful pot without drainage holes, use it as a cachepot — place your plant in a plain nursery pot inside the decorative one. Just remember to empty any accumulated water after watering.

## Material matters

### Terracotta

**Best for:** succulents, cacti, Mediterranean herbs, plants prone to overwatering
**Pros:** porous, allows roots to breathe, moisture evaporates through the walls
**Cons:** dries out faster — you'll need to water more frequently
**Look:** rustic, traditional, warm

### Plastic

**Best for:** moisture-loving plants, ferns, calathea, peace lily
**Pros:** retains moisture longer, lightweight, inexpensive
**Cons:** doesn't breathe, can stay wet too long for drought-tolerant plants
**Look:** functional, available in many colours

### Glazed ceramic

**Best for:** decorative display, plants with moderate water needs
**Pros:** retains moisture like plastic, but more decorative
**Cons:** heavier, less breathable than terracotta
**Look:** elegant, wide range of styles

### Fabric grow bags

**Best for:** fast-growing plants, temporary growing, excellent drainage
**Pros:** air-prunes roots (prevents root circling), dries out evenly, lightweight
**Cons:** not the most decorative, dries out quickly
**Look:** utilitarian

## Size: when bigger is not better

A common mistake is choosing a pot that's much too large for the plant. Extra soil stays wet for weeks after watering — and standing in waterlogged soil leads to root rot.

**Rule of thumb:** when repotting, choose a new pot only 2–4 cm wider in diameter than the current one.

Signs it's time to repot:

- Roots growing out of the drainage hole
- Roots circling the inside of the pot
- Soil drying out very quickly after watering
- The plant seems disproportionately large for its container

**When not to repot:** in autumn or winter, when plants are in their rest phase. Spring is ideal.

## Shape and depth

- **Deep pots** suit plants with long taproots — palms, dracaena, ficus
- **Shallow pots** work well for succulents and plants with shallow root systems
- **Wide, shallow bowls** are great for terrariums or group plantings

## Self-watering pots

Self-watering pots have a reservoir at the bottom and a wicking system that draws water up to the roots as needed. They're excellent for:

- Holiday absences
- Moisture-loving plants like peace lily and ferns
- Busy plant owners who tend to forget watering

They are **not** suitable for succulents, cacti or plants that need to dry out completely between waterings.

## Practical tips

- **Match material to plant needs** — terracotta for cacti and succulents, plastic or glazed ceramic for tropical moisture-lovers
- **Start smaller** — a snug pot is safer than a large one
- **Add a layer of gravel at the bottom?** — this old advice is a myth. It actually raises the water table inside the pot. Skip it and use well-draining soil instead.
- **Saucers** — use them to protect surfaces, but empty them after 30 minutes to avoid root rot

> _The right pot is the one that supports your plant's natural habits — it drains when it should, holds moisture when it should, and gives roots just enough room to grow._
