Starting your plant journey is one of the most rewarding things you can do for your home and your wellbeing. But it can feel overwhelming at first — so many species, so many different needs. This guide will get you started the right way.

## Choosing your first plant

The biggest mistake beginners make is choosing a plant that looks beautiful in the shop without considering whether it suits their home environment.

Instead, start by asking yourself:

- **How much light do I have?** Most homes have moderate indirect light.
- **How often do I want to water?** Be honest — if you travel often, choose something drought-tolerant.
- **What size plant do I want?** A large statement plant or a compact windowsill collection?

### Great starter plants

**For low-light homes:** ZZ plant, pothos, peace lily, snake plant
**For forgetful waterers:** ZZ plant, snake plant, cactus, aloe vera
**For bright rooms:** monstera, rubber plant, dracaena
**For fast growth and reward:** pothos, spider plant, philodendron

## The right pot and soil

### Drainage is everything

The most important feature of any pot is a drainage hole. Without one, water pools at the bottom and causes root rot — the number one killer of houseplants.

If you love a decorative pot without a drainage hole, use it as a cachepot (outer pot) with a plain nursery pot inside.

### Choosing soil

Standard potting compost works for most leafy houseplants. But specialised mixes make a real difference:

- **Succulent and cactus mix** — gritty and fast-draining
- **Orchid bark** — chunky, airy mix for orchids
- **Peat-free potting compost** — for ferns, calathea and moisture-loving plants

## The three fundamentals

Once you have your plant in the right pot with good soil, success comes down to three things:

### 1. Light

Place your plant where it gets the right amount of light for its species. Most tropical houseplants want bright indirect light — near a window but not in direct sun.

### 2. Watering

The finger test is your best tool: press your finger 2–3 cm into the soil. Water only when the top layer is dry. More plants die from overwatering than underwatering.

### 3. Temperature and humidity

Most houseplants come from tropical environments and prefer temperatures above 15°C and humidity above 40%. Avoid placing them near radiators, draughty windows or air conditioning vents.

## Building confidence

Plant care is a skill you build over time. Every plant teaches you something new. Here's how to grow your confidence:

- **Observe your plants daily** — you'll start to notice subtle changes before they become problems
- **Keep a simple plant diary** — note when you water, when you repot, any issues you notice
- **Start with forgiving species** — let yourself succeed before tackling demanding plants
- **Don't be afraid of failure** — even experienced growers lose plants sometimes

## Common beginner questions

**"My leaves are yellowing — what's wrong?"**
Usually overwatering. Check the soil moisture and reduce watering frequency.

**"My plant looks healthy but isn't growing."**
Often insufficient light, or it's the dormant season. Move it closer to a window.

**"I found tiny flies in my soil."**
Fungus gnats — very common. Let the soil dry out more between waterings, or use yellow sticky traps.

**"When should I repot?"**
When roots are growing out of the drainage hole, or the plant seems to be drying out much faster than usual. Spring is the best time.
