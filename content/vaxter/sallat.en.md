Lettuce was cultivated in ancient Egypt and Persia more than 4,500 years ago and has since become a staple crop in almost every part of the world. There are hundreds of varieties in all shapes and flavours — from soft butterhead to peppery rocket and crisp romaine.

## Popular Lettuce Varieties

- **Butterhead** – Loosely formed head with soft, buttery leaves and a mild flavour. Easy to grow and tolerant.
- **Romaine / Cos** – Upright, crisp leaves and firm head. The foundation of a classic Caesar salad.
- **Lollo Rosso** – Curly, red-edged leaves and a slight bitterness. Decorative and delicious.
- **Iceberg** – Firm, crisp head and neutral flavour. Requires more space and time than loose-leaf types.
- **Oak Leaf** – Loose head with oak-leaf shaped leaves. Mild and delicate — excellent cut-and-come-again lettuce.
- **Frisée** – Curly, divided leaf structure with a slight bitterness. Popular in French salads.

## Care

### Light

Lettuce tolerates and thrives in partial shade to indirect light — one of the few vegetables that does not require full sun. Strong direct summer sun can make lettuce bitter and cause it to bolt.

### Watering

Keep the soil evenly moist but not waterlogged. Lettuce has a shallow root system and dries out quickly. Water little and often — preferably in the morning.

### Feeding

Lettuce is not a heavy feeder — a little balanced fertiliser every three weeks is sufficient. Over-fertilising with nitrogen produces watery, flavourless leaves.

### Temperature and Bolting

Lettuce thrives best at 15–20 °C. At temperatures above 25 °C it bolts rapidly — shooting up a flower stalk and the leaves become bitter. Choose a heat-tolerant variety for the hottest summer periods, or shade the plant.

### Succession Sowing

Sow a small amount of lettuce every 2–3 weeks during the season to always have a fresh harvest. This is called succession growing.

## Harvest

Pick outer leaves one by one and leave the heart intact — the plant continues to grow and provides a harvest for weeks. Harvest early in the morning when the leaves are firm from the night's cool.
