Dracaena marginata — often simply called "dracaena" in garden centres — is one of the most beautiful and structurally striking houseplants. The thin, arching leaves with red or purple edges sit in dense rosettes on slender, cork-like stems that branch over the years to create a unique, almost sculptural silhouette.

## Origin

Dracaena marginata originates from Madagascar, where it grows in dry, rocky terrain under intermittent rain seasons. It is a hardened plant adapted to adversity — and this shows clearly in how it handles indoor environments.

## Light

Dracaena does best in moderate to bright indirect light. Place it 1–2 metres from a bright window. Varieties with reddish or purple leaves need more light to retain their colours. Dracaena tolerates semi-shade but grows more slowly and may lose its characteristic colouring. Direct sun causes bleached, brown leaf patches.

## Watering

Water when the top 3–5 cm of the soil surface has dried out — usually every two to three weeks. Dracaena tolerates some dryness but doesn't like to stay wet. In winter, water more sparingly.

**Important:** Dracaena is sensitive to fluoride and boron in tap water. Use filtered, distilled, or collected rainwater if possible. Brown leaf tips are the clearest sign of fluoride damage.

## Shaping & Height

Dracaena can grow quite tall over the years. To control height or create branching, simply cut the stem at the desired height with a sharp, clean knife. One or two new shoots typically emerge from the cut — a good way to create a bushier plant. The cut tops can be used as cuttings.

## Air Purification

NASA's Clean Air Study identified dracaena as one of the more effective air-purifying houseplants — it filters formaldehyde, benzene, xylene, and trichloroethylene. A typical office and home favourite for exactly this reason.

## Popular Varieties

- **'Marginata'** — standard form with green leaves and red edges
- **'Tricolor'** — green leaves with yellow and red stripes, more vivid
- **'Colorama'** — intensely red-pink colouring, needs more light
- **'Bicolor'** — green-red two-tone variant

## Toxicity

Dracaena is toxic to dogs and cats. Symptoms from ingestion include vomiting, salivation, and weakness. Keep out of reach of pets.

## Common Problems

- **Brown leaf tips** — almost always fluoride sensitivity; switch to filtered water
- **Yellow or brownish patches** — overwatering or direct sun
- **Leaves lose colour** — too little light; move to a brighter position
- **Soft stem** — rot caused by overwatering; check roots and act immediately
