Few plants inspire as much admiration as a well-tended calathea. The round, broad leaves with their intricate patterns — silver-banded, zebra-striped, velvety purple undersides — are one of nature's most spectacular visual achievements. Calathea is not the easiest plant to keep, but given the right environment it rewards its owner with a beauty that surpasses most.

## Origin

Calatheas are natural inhabitants of the deepest parts of tropical rainforests in South and Central America — Brazil, Ecuador, Colombia. There they grow on the forest floor under a dense canopy, in constant high humidity, diffuse light, and steady warmth. These are exactly the conditions we need to recreate indoors.

## Night Movement

One of calathea's most fascinating features is its _nyctinastic movement_ — the leaves fold upward at night (like hands in prayer, hence the nickname "prayer plant") and open again in the morning toward the light. It's a living, moving presence in the home.

## Light

Calathea needs **moderate, indirect light** — no direct sun. It does best near a north- or east-facing window, or further into a well-lit room. Direct sun quickly burns the leaves and bleaches the patterning.

## Watering

Keep the soil evenly moist but never soggy. Water when the top 2 cm of the surface has dried out. Calathea is sensitive to minerals in tap water — calcium, fluoride, and chlorine can cause brown leaf edges and tips. Use **distilled water**, **rainwater**, or **tap water left open for 24 hours** (chlorine evaporates).

## Humidity — The Critical Requirement

Calathea needs **high humidity**, ideally 60 % or more. Dry indoor air in winter is the most common problem. Solutions include placing the plant on a tray of pebbles and water, using a nearby humidifier, grouping it with other moisture-loving plants, or positioning it in a bathroom or kitchen with natural humidity.

## Temperature

Calathea thrives at 18–27 °C and cannot tolerate temperatures below 15 °C. Avoid drafts, cold windows, and placement near air-conditioning units.

## Popular Varieties

- **Calathea orbifolia** — round, silver-striped leaves; the most popular and slightly more tolerant
- **Calathea zebrina** — bold green and yellow stripes
- **Calathea medallion** — dark green leaves with a rose pattern and purple underside
- **Calathea ornata** — narrow pink and white lines on dark green leaves — truly dramatic
- **Calathea lancifolia** — elongated leaves with a zigzag pattern (rattlesnake plant)

## Common Problems

- **Rolled leaves** — classic sign of dry air or underwatering
- **Brown leaf edges** — mineral-rich water, dry air, or cold water — switch to distilled water
- **Loss of patterning** — too much light; move further from the window
