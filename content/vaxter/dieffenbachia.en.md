Dieffenbachia looks expensive and works as green architecture in a room: sturdy stems, large beautifully patterned leaves. With moderate care it grows into an impressive specimen.

## Origin and natural habitat

Dieffenbachia comes from tropical forests in the Caribbean and South America, where it grows in humid understory. The name honours Austrian gardener Joseph Dieffenbach.

## Care

### Light

Bright indirect light keeps the pattern at its best. It tolerates half shade, but dim spots turn the leaves green and leggy. Avoid direct sun.

### Watering

Let the top centimetre dry between waterings. Dieffenbachia likes an even keel — neither dry nor soggy.

### Soil and feeding

Humus-rich, free-draining potting soil. Feed fortnightly in summer. Prune tall stems in spring — they branch readily.

## Common problems

**Yellow leaves** most often signal overwatering. **Drooping leaves** come from drought or cold. **Dropping lower leaves** is normal as it grows tall — bare stems can be cut back.

## Good to know

The sap is strongly irritating and the plant was historically called "dumb cane" — it temporarily robbed chewers of speech. Handle with gloves and keep away from children and pets.
