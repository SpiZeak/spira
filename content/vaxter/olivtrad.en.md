Few plants breathe holiday like a potted olive tree. The silvery foliage and characterful trunk feel equally at home on a balcony or in the living room.

## Origin and natural habitat

The olive has been cultivated around the Mediterranean for over 6,000 years and is one of humanity's oldest crop plants. Wild populations originate in the eastern Mediterranean, where it endures summer drought on stony slopes.

## Care

### Light

Olive trees want as much sun as possible — a south-facing window or patio. Put it outside from May to September once frost risk has passed.

### Watering

Let the soil dry out properly between waterings. Olives tolerate drought well but dislike wet feet — make sure the pot drains.

### Soil and feeding

Sandy, chalky, free-draining soil. Feed sparingly with lime-rich fertiliser in summer. Overwinter cool (5–10 °C) and bright, watering rarely then.

## Common problems

**Leaf drop** usually comes from soil that is too wet or a too-dark winter spot. **Aphids** sometimes appear in spring — rinse off with water. **No fruit** is normal indoors; olives need cross-pollination and abundant light.

## Good to know

Olive trees can live for over a thousand years. Safe for cats and dogs.
