Delphiniums are one of the summer garden's most impressive perennials. The 1–2 metre tall flower spikes covered in blue-violet, sky-blue, lavender-pink, or white flowers create a vertical drama that few other plants can match. They are the centrepiece of the classic English cottage garden and equally at home in traditional Scandinavian farmhouse borders.

## Varieties

**Pacific Giants** are the most common delphiniums in garden centres — large, double flowers in intense colours. They are short-lived (2–3 years) but give a magnificent effect. **Belladonna group** (_D. × belladonna_) — more branched, elegant plants with looser flower spikes. More hardy and longer-lived. **Elatum hybrids** are the classic tall hybrids with their characteristic dense flower spikes.

## Light & Wind Protection

Delphinium needs full sun. Plant against a protected wall or hedge, or provide strong stakes — the tall stems break easily in strong wind.

## Soil

Deep, rich, well-draining, and preferably neutral to slightly alkaline soil. Delphinium loves lime — unlike lupines and rhododendron. Improve generously with compost at planting time.

## Watering

Water regularly and deeply during flowering. Delphinium has deep roots and tolerates shorter dry spells once established, but flowers less well without regular watering.

## Feeding

Fertilise generously with an all-purpose fertiliser or horn meal in spring, and with a flowering fertiliser during blooming. Delphinium is a heavy feeder.

## Staking — Obligatory

Insert robust stakes (bamboo or steel rods) early in the season — before the plant needs them — and tie stems loosely with garden twine. A gust of wind can snap stems at the peak of flowering.

## After Flowering

Cut the flower spikes down to about 10–15 cm after flowering in June–July. The plant usually produces new flower spikes giving a second, if somewhat weaker, bloom in September.

## Overwintering

Delphinium is hardy in zones 1–3 without extra protection. In colder zones, cover the base with conifer branches.

## Toxicity

All parts of delphinium are toxic and can cause serious poisoning if ingested — especially seeds and young shoots. Take care with delphiniums in gardens frequented by children and animals.

## Common Problems

**Powdery mildew** is the most common problem and appears in dry, warm weather. Choose airy positions and avoid evening watering.

**Aphids** can attack young shoots in large numbers. Treat with insecticidal soap.

**Short-lived plant** is normal for Pacific Giants — renew regularly with seed sowing or new plants.
