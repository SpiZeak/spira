The spider plant is a classic houseplant — beloved by generations for its easy-care nature, beautiful arching leaves, and the charming miniature plantlets that hang in long threads below the mother plant. It is a plant that gives back more than you put in.

## Origin

Chlorophytum comosum originates from the subtropical and tropical forests of southern Africa. There it grows as a ground cover under trees, adapted to variable light and intermittent rainfall. These traits explain why it copes so well in indoor environments with irregular watering and variable light conditions.

## Light

The spider plant does best in moderate to bright indirect light but handles semi-shade well. Variegated varieties (with white or yellow stripes) need a little more light to retain their colouring. Direct sun causes brown, scorched leaf tips.

## Watering

Water regularly and keep the soil evenly moist during summer. The spider plant tolerates some drying out but does best with regular watering. In winter, reduce watering — the plant is resting. The leaf tips can turn brown if the water contains too much fluoride or chlorine. Use filtered water or let tap water stand for a day before watering.

## The Runners — A Home for Babies

One of the spider plant's most beloved features is its "baby plants" (stolons) — long, arched stems with miniature plantlets at the tips. These can be left hanging for a dramatic effect in a hanging basket, or cut off and planted in their own pots in moist soil. Roots form quickly and it is one of the easiest ways to propagate a plant.

## Placement

The spider plant is fantastic in a hanging basket at about 1–2 metres height, where the runners can hang freely. It also does well on a high bookshelf or pedestal. Avoid drafts and cold positions in winter.

## Air Purification

The spider plant is on NASA's list of air-purifying plants and has been shown to effectively absorb formaldehyde and xylene — common substances from furniture, textiles, and cleaning products.

## Popular Varieties

- **'Vittatum'** — classic variety with white central stripe on green leaves
- **'Variegatum'** — green leaves with white edges (reverse marking to Vittatum)
- **'Bonnie'** — compact, curly and wavy leaves — exquisitely decorative
- **Green form** — solid green leaves, more robust, somewhat less ornamental

## Common Problems

**Brown leaf tips** — almost universally normal for spider plants. Most common causes: fluoride/chlorine in tap water, dry air, dry soil. Trim the brown tips along the leaf's natural shape.

**White patches on leaves** — sun or water damage. Move away from direct sun.

**No runners** — the plant needs to become a little more established and pot-bound, or needs more light, to produce runners.
