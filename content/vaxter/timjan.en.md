Thyme is equally at home between paving slabs and in a pot: a drought-tolerant groundcover with intense fragrance. Bees love the flowers, cooks love the leaves.

## Origin and natural habitat

Thyme comes from the Mediterranean's dry hillsides, where it forms fragrant mats on stony ground. Its aromatic oils protect against drought — and underpin the herb's medicinal reputation.

## Care

### Light

Full sun gives the most fragrance and tightest growth. Indoors: a bright south window. Excellent in pots or gravel gardens.

### Watering

Let the soil dry out completely between waterings. Thyme is semi-succulent and tolerates drought better than most — it dies of loving overwatering sooner than neglect.

### Soil and feeding

Sandy, lean, sharply drained soil. Feed barely at all. After flowering: shear back by half for a dense cushion. Dried sprigs keep their scent for months.

## Common problems

**Rot in the centre** means too damp and crowded — thin and prune. **Grey foliage** after winter: shear off in spring. **Leggy growth** indoors means low light.

## Good to know

The Egyptians used thyme in embalming, and in antiquity it was burned as incense. Thymol is still used in mouthwash today. Safe for cats and dogs.
