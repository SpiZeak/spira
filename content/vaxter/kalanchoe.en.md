Kalanchoe is the succulent world's splash of colour: water-storing fleshy leaves combined with weeks of flowers. The perfect sunny-window plant for the forgetful.

## Origin and natural habitat

Kalanchoe blossfeldiana was found growing wild on Madagascar's dry mountain slopes, where it flowers after rainy periods. As a succulent it is built to survive long dry spells.

## Care

### Light

A bright spot with a few hours of direct morning sun gives the most abundant flowering. More light means more buds.

### Watering

Water thoroughly but rarely — every two to three weeks, and always empty the saucer. The leaves should never stay wet.

### Soil and feeding

Cactus or succulent soil is ideal. Feed sparingly while flowering. Deadhead spent flowers for reblooming; for a second flush the plant needs six weeks of short days.

## Common problems

**Soft, translucent leaves** mean overwatering — the most common mistake. **Leggy, sparse growth** comes from too little light. **No reblooming** requires short-day treatment, just like a poinsettia.

## Good to know

Kalanchoe can flower for up to eight weeks straight. Note: toxic to cats and dogs.
