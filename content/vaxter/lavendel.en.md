Lavender's silver-shimmering foliage and blue-purple flower spikes have made it one of the most sought-after garden perennials in Scandinavia. Its clear fragrance, which is calming and timeless, attracts bees and butterflies in abundance and makes lavender an excellent choice for pollinator-friendly gardens.

## Hardiness in Scandinavia

_Lavandula angustifolia_ — true lavender — is the hardiest species and tolerates winters in zones 1–3 in Scandinavia with the right conditions. The key is well-draining soil: lavender tolerates cold but not wet soil during winter. Cultivars such as 'Hidcote', 'Munstead', and 'Vera' are proven hardy in Scandinavian conditions.

## Light & Placement

Lavender requires full sun — at least 6–8 hours of direct sunlight per day. Choose the warmest, sunniest position in the garden, ideally against a south-facing wall that stores heat. A slight slope or raised bed improves drainage.

## Soil

Good drainage is absolutely paramount. Lavender thrives best in lean, lime-rich, sandy soil. In heavy clay soil, work in plenty of grit or perlite. Avoid nutrient-rich, heavily fertilised soil — it produces lush but short-lived plants.

## Watering

Lavender is distinctly drought-tolerant once established. Water newly planted specimens regularly during the first year. Thereafter, normal rainfall is sufficient in most Scandinavian gardens, except during extremely dry summers.

## Pruning — Critical for Long Life

Pruning is lavender's most important care task. Trim back flowering stems immediately after flowering and do a harder shaping cut in spring (April) when new green shoots begin to appear. Never cut back into the old woody stem — lavender does not produce new shoots from old wood.

## Overwintering

In the more northern parts of Scandinavia (zones 3–4), give lavender some winter protection of conifer branches or leaves. Remove the protection early in spring to prevent moisture from accumulating.

## Harvesting & Uses

Harvest flower spikes just when about half the flowers have opened — the fragrance is strongest and shelf life longest at that stage. Dry tied bunches upside down in a dark, airy space. Dried lavender is used in sachets against moths in wardrobes, in cooking, and as décor.

## Common Problems

**Root rot** is lavender's worst enemy and always results from soil that is too wet. Ensure drainage and avoid watering too often.

**Woody, sparse growth** with few flowers is a sign that pruning has been neglected. Lavenders that aren't cut back regularly quickly become woody and short-lived.
