Syngonium is happy to grow almost anywhere and cascades over a shelf edge within six months. White and pink varieties add extra colour.

## Origin and natural habitat

Syngonium podophyllum climbs through tropical forests from Mexico to Ecuador. As a juvenile it creeps along the ground with pointed leaves; as a mature plant it climbs with lobed leaves toward the light — a trait called heterophylly.

## Care

### Light

Indirect light is optimal, but syngonium tolerates dimmer spots too (with darker green leaves). Avoid direct sun that bleaches.

### Watering

Water when the top centimetre dries, usually weekly. It forgives a missed watering without drama.

### Soil and feeding

Ordinary potting soil with perlite. Feed fortnightly in summer. Prune long trails for a bushier habit — cuttings root easily in water.

## Common problems

**Yellow leaves** usually mean overwatering. **Long, sparse trails** signal too little light. **Small, pale leaves** point to nutrient deficiency during the growing season.

## Good to know

Leaf shapes change with age — a single plant can carry both pointed juvenile leaves and lobed mature ones. Note: toxic.
