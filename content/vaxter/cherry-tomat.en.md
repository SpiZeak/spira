The cherry tomato is a favourite among balcony and container gardeners. The small, sugar-sweet fruits ripen in dense clusters and provide an abundant harvest throughout the whole summer. With the right care, a single plant can produce hundreds of tomatoes per season.

## Origin and History

The tomato originates from western South America, most likely Peru and Ecuador, and was cultivated by the Aztecs and Incas long before Europeans arrived. Cherry tomatoes are considered closely related to the original wild tomatoes, which all bore small, round fruit.

## Popular Cherry Varieties

- **Sweet Million** – Abundantly sweet, red berries in long trusses.
- **Sungold** – Irresistible orange cherry tomato with fruity, tropical sweetness.
- **Black Cherry** – Dark red to brownish colour with a complex, rich flavour.
- **Yellow Pear** – Pear-shaped yellow tomato, mild and sweet — beautiful in salads.

## Care

### Light

Tomatoes need at least 6–8 hours of direct sunlight per day. Place in the sunniest south-facing window, or grow outdoors on a sunny balcony.

### Watering

Water regularly and evenly — uneven watering leads to blossom-end rot (black patch on the bottom of the fruit). Water at the base of the plant and avoid wetting the leaves to prevent fungal diseases. Check daily during hot summer days.

### Feeding

Start feeding with tomato fertiliser (high-potassium) once the first flower truss opens. Feed weekly throughout the season.

### Staking and Support

Cherry tomatoes are usually indeterminate (climbing) and need support — a bamboo cane or tomato cage. Pinch out side shoots that form in the leaf axils to keep the plant tidy and direct energy toward fruit set.

### Soil & Pot

Use a nutrient-rich potting mix or blend standard potting soil with compost. Choose a pot of at least 10–15 litres. Good drainage is essential.

## Common Problems

**Blossom-end rot** (black-brown patch on the fruit) is caused by calcium deficiency due to uneven watering. Water more consistently.

**Aphids** tend to cluster on young shoots. Rinse off with water or spray with an organic insecticide.

**Yellow leaves lower down** are normal as the plant ages, but can also indicate nitrogen deficiency.

## Harvest

Harvest when the fruits are fully coloured and give slightly when gently squeezed. Cherry tomatoes hold well on the plant, but taste best freshly picked.
