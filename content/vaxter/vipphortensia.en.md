The panicle hydrangea (_Hydrangea paniculata_) is for many the best hydrangea for Nordic gardens. Its hardiness is superior — well-cared-for specimens handle zones 4–5 without problems — and its blooming season is impressively long, from July right into October. What makes it especially decorative is that the white to cream-yellow panicles gradually turn pink during autumn, creating a two-toned drama against yellowing leaves.

## Popular Cultivars

**'Limelight'** — probably the best-selling panicle hydrangea in Scandinavia. Blooms in lime-yellow-white and turns beautifully pink. Upright habit up to 200 cm.

**'Vanille Fraise'** — starts white and transitions to deep strawberry-pink. More compact and graceful in form.

**'Little Lime'** — a lower-growing variant of 'Limelight', suitable for smaller gardens and containers.

**'Pinky Winky'** — two-toned panicles with white flowers at the top and pink at the base. Dramatic effect.

**'Phantom'** — one of the largest cultivars with enormous 30 cm panicles. Needs staking.

## Light

Panicle hydrangea thrives best in sun to partial shade. Unlike bigleaf hydrangea it tolerates more sun. In moist positions with full sun it flowers most abundantly.

## Watering

Keep the soil evenly moist during the establishment period (first 1–2 years). Established shrubs are relatively drought-tolerant but flower better with regular watering during dry periods.

## Feeding

Fertilise in spring with a general fertiliser or one designed for flowering shrubs. An extra application in June prolongs flowering. Avoid fertilising after July, as the plant then needs to prepare for winter.

## Pruning

_H. paniculata_ blooms on the current season's new growth, making it easy to prune. Cut all branches down to 50–70 cm in spring (April) — the harder you cut, the fewer but larger flower clusters. Never prune in autumn; the old flower stalks provide winter interest and protect the buds from frost.

## Overwintering

Panicle hydrangea is hardy and needs no special protection in most parts of Scandinavia. In zone 5, a little brushwood or leaves around the base during the first winter is wise.

## Planting

Plant in a partially shaded to sunny position in well-draining but moisture-retentive soil. Add plenty of compost at planting time. Allow 150–200 cm between shrubs when planting as a hedge.

## Common Problems

**Drooping flower clusters** despite staking often results from varieties with heavy panicles (like 'Phantom') — position the shrub in a wind-sheltered spot and stake early during flowering.

**Brown-patched leaves** can be caused by too much sun, water shortage, or fungal infection. Ensure even watering and good air circulation.

## Fun Fact

_Hydrangea paniculata_ is one of the few hydrangea species that can grow as a full-sized tree (up to 6 m) if allowed to. In Japan it is sometimes used as a street tree. The name "paniculata" comes from the Latin _panicula_, referring to the cone-shaped structure of the flower (a panicle).
