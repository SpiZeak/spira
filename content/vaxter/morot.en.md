The carrot originates from Afghanistan and Central Asia and was originally cultivated for its aromatic seeds and leaves — not the root. Purple carrots were bred to orange in the Netherlands in the 17th century, possibly as a tribute to William of Orange, and the orange variety dominates the world market to this day.

## Popular Carrot Varieties

- **Nantes** – Cylindrical, uniformly thick and juicy. Classic salad carrot with excellent sweetness. Perfect for pots.
- **Chantenay** – Short and broad-shouldered. Handles more compact and heavier soils. Excellent for storage.
- **Danvers** – Cone-shaped, sturdy variety that manages slightly heavier soil. A good all-round carrot.
- **Scarlet Nantes** – Crunchy, sweet and early. One of the most popular heirloom varieties.
- **Rainbow Mix** – Blend of red, yellow, white and purple carrots. Spectacularly beautiful in crudités and salads.

## Care

### Sowing

Carrots are direct-sown outdoors — they do not like transplanting. Sow thinly in rows and then thin to 5–8 cm spacing when the seedlings are about 5 cm tall. Thinned seedlings can be used in salads!

The soil should be at least 10 °C at sowing — from April–May in Sweden.

### Light

Carrots do best in full sun but tolerate partial shade. Foliage growth needs light; the root primarily needs warmth and nutrients from the soil.

### Soil

Loose, deep and sandy or sandy-loam soil free of stones — stones cause carrots to split and fork. In a container, use a deep pot (at least 30–40 cm deep) with porous potting mix.

### Watering

Water deeply and regularly. Uneven watering causes split roots or cracked surfaces. During dry periods — water generously but infrequently rather than a little and often.

### Feeding

Avoid fresh manure directly in the planting bed (causes split, hairy roots). Well-rotted compost works best.

## Harvest

Carrots are ready to harvest when the shoulders (top of the root) are protruding about 1–2 cm from the soil and have reached the desired thickness. Taste one — home-grown carrots are sweet and crisp far beyond what shop carrots can offer. Autumn-harvested carrots after a light frost are especially sweet.
