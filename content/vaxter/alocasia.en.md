Alocasia is sculptural as album art: dark, pointed leaves with white veins that look hand-drawn. It makes demands — but few plants make such a strong interior statement.

## Origin and natural habitat

The Alocasia genus comprises about 80 species in tropical Asia, from India to New Guinea. 'Polly' is a cultivated hybrid close to Alocasia longiloba, whose relatives cool shaded forest streams in their native range.

## Care

### Light

Bright indirect light gives the best foliage and colour. Too little light yields small, green leaves; direct sun scorches.

### Watering

Keep the soil lightly moist but never soggy, and water less in winter when the plant often rests. Alocasia wilts theatrically from both drought and overwatering.

### Soil and feeding

Airy, coarse soil with bark and perlite. Feed fortnightly during growth. A winter rest with cooler temperatures and sparse watering yields stronger spring shoots.

## Common problems

**Yellow leaves** are the most common worry and stem from overwatering, cold, or winter rest. **Spider mites** enjoy dry air. **All leaves dropping in winter** is often normal dormancy — let the root rest until spring.

## Good to know

Alocasia is called elephant's ear for its huge leaves — in the wild, leaves of some species grow over two metres wide. Note: toxic.
