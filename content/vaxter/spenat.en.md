Spinach originates from Persia (modern-day Iran) and spread westward via Arab trade routes during the Middle Ages. It reached Europe in the 10th century and was a highly valued vegetable at European courts during the Renaissance. Spinach is known for its high iron content — but note that the iron in spinach is non-haem iron, which the body absorbs less efficiently than the haem iron in meat.

## Popular Spinach Varieties

- **Bloomsdale** – Dark green, crinkled, thick leaves with excellent flavour. Heirloom and a home-grower's favourite.
- **Baby Leaf / Tyee** – Soft, slender leaves perfect for salads and smoothies. Fast-growing.
- **Giant Nobel** – Large, flat-leafed variety with a mild flavour. Popular for cooking and sautéing.
- **Perpetual Spinach (Chard)** – Actually a chard variety, but tastes and looks like spinach with a longer season and more heat tolerance.
- **New Zealand Spinach** – Heat-tolerant "spinach" (different species) that thrives in summer heat without bolting. Perfect alternative during July–August.

## Care

### Light

Spinach thrives in partial shade and is one of the best vegetables for shaded balconies and north-facing windowsills. Direct, strong summer sun causes it to bolt.

### Watering

Keep soil evenly moist. Spinach has a shallow root system and dries out quickly — water little and often. Drought produces thin, tough and bitter leaves.

### Feeding

Nitrogen-rich fertiliser stimulates leaf growth. Feed lightly every three weeks with a balanced fertiliser.

### Temperature and Bolting

Spinach thrives at 10–20 °C and is a typical spring and autumn crop. At temperatures above 25 °C it bolts and the leaves become bitter. Choose "slow bolt" varieties for a longer season.

### Succession Sowing

Sow every two to three weeks for continuous harvest from April to November (with the right variety selection).

## Harvest

Pick outer leaves with scissors or by hand when they have reached 8–10 cm and leave the heart — the plant continues to produce. You can also harvest the whole plant at ground level — it regrows if you leave a few leaves behind. Spinach is excellent steamed, sautéed in butter with garlic, or eaten raw in smoothies.
