Hydrangea has undergone a remarkable popularity journey in Scandinavia. Once regarded as a stuffy funeral shrub, it is today one of the most purchased garden shrubs in the region — and rightly so. Its enormous flower clusters in pastel shades, long blooming season, and ability to cope with partial shade make it unique among garden plants.

## Varieties for Scandinavian Gardens

**Smooth hydrangea** (_Hydrangea arborescens_) — with the classic 'Annabelle' — is the hardiest and tolerates zone 4. Blooms on the current season's growth in large white globes.

**Panicle hydrangea** (_Hydrangea paniculata_) is also very hardy (zone 4–5). Cultivars like 'Limelight' and 'Vanille Fraise' bloom July–October with conical white-to-pink panicles.

**Bigleaf hydrangea** (_Hydrangea macrophylla_) — the classic blue and pink mopheads — is the most beautiful but least hardy (zone 1–2). Requires winter protection in colder parts of Scandinavia.

## Blue or Pink — Soil pH Decides

A fascinating property of _H. macrophylla_ is that flower colour is influenced by soil pH and aluminium availability. Acid soil (pH below 6) with available aluminium gives blue flowers; lime-rich, neutral soil (pH 6–7) gives pink. White varieties are unaffected by pH.

## Care

### Light

Hydrangea thrives best in partial shade — morning sun with protection from hot midday sun is ideal. Full sun produces brown-patched leaves and dries the plant out quickly. One of the few garden shrubs that blooms well in shadier positions.

### Watering

Hydrangea's large foliage transpires a great deal of water. Water generously and regularly, ideally daily during hot periods. Never let the plant dry out completely — it signals with drooping leaves but can be damaged. Mulch around the base to retain moisture.

### Feeding

Fertilise with a flowering fertiliser (rich in potassium and phosphorus) in May and again in June. Avoid nitrogen fertiliser late in the season.

### Pruning

The correct pruning time depends on the species: _H. macrophylla_ blooms on the previous year's growth — only remove dead and weak branches immediately after flowering. _H. paniculata_ and _H. arborescens_ bloom on the current season's growth and can be pruned hard in spring.

### Overwintering

Protect bigleaf hydrangea with a brush-wood screen or mound up with leaves in November. Remove protection in April.

## Common Problems

**Drooping leaves despite watering** can result from root damage or disease. Check that the roots are not damaged by ants or water shoot growth.

**Failure to flower** is most common with _H. macrophylla_ and usually results from over-pruning that removed the flower buds, or frost damage to the previous year's shoots.
