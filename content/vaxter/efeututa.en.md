Pothos is sometimes called "the impossible to kill" — and not without reason. This climbing vine is known for its ability to survive conditions that would test other plants' patience: dark corners, forgotten watering, dry air. And yet it flourishes.

## Origin

Epipremnum aureum originates from the tropical rainforests of the Solomon Islands in the Pacific Ocean, but has spread as a naturalised plant across much of Southeast Asia, South America, and parts of Australia. In the wild it climbs along trees and can grow many metres long. It is worth noting that pothos is considered an invasive species in several regions due to its aggressive growth — a testament to how extraordinarily adaptable it truly is.

## Light

Pothos tolerates a wide spectrum of light conditions — from bright indirect light to relatively shaded positions. Variegated varieties (with white or yellow leaf markings) need more light to retain their colouring. In darker environments the leaves tend to revert to solid green. Avoid direct sun — it burns the leaves.

## Watering

Water when the top 3–4 cm of soil has dried out. Pothos manages without water longer than most plants, but does best with regular watering. Yellow leaves are the clearest sign of overwatering.

## Shaping & Training

One of pothos's great advantages is how easily it can be shaped. Trim back long vines at any time — the cuttings can be placed in a glass of water and form roots within a few weeks, giving you a free new plant.

With a support — a moss pole, bamboo, or trellis — pothos climbs upward and produces increasingly larger leaves. Without support, the vines trail naturally downward, making it perfect in a hanging basket.

## Toxicity

Note that pothos is toxic to dogs and cats, and can cause irritation in the mouth and throat if eaten by children. Keep out of reach of pets and small children.

## Popular Varieties

- **'Golden Pothos'** — classic variety with yellow-green leaves
- **'Marble Queen'** — chalk-white marbled leaves, needs more light
- **'Neon Pothos'** — brilliant lime-green leaves
- **'N'joy'** — compact variety with clear white-green variegation
