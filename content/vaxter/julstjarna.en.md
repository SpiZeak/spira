No plant says December like the poinsettia. With proper care the colourful bracts last from early December well into the new year.

## Origin and natural habitat

Euphorbia pulcherrima grows wild in Mexico's dry mountain regions, where it can reach several metres. The Aztecs used its red bracts as dye — its career as a Christmas plant began in 1920s California.

## Care

### Light

Place it bright, ideally in a cool window, but avoid both direct midday sun and draughts. The brighter the spot, the longer the bracts last.

### Watering

Let the soil dry slightly between waterings and use tepid water. Cold water and cold feet make leaves drop — poinsettias hate chill.

### Soil and feeding

Ordinary potting soil. Do not feed while in bloom. To re-colour bracts for next year it needs twelve hours of complete darkness per day for eight weeks from October.

## Common problems

**Leaf drop** is the classic complaint and comes from cold transport, draughts, or cold water. **Yellowing bracts** mean too warm or too dark. **Whitefly** can appear — yellow sticky traps help.

## Good to know

The red "flower" is really transformed leaves; the true flowers are the small yellow buds in the centre. The sap is mildly toxic to pets.
