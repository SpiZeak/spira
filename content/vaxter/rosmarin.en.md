Rosemary smells of holidays in Provence and is one of the toughest herbs. Happy in a pot, overwintered cool, it supplies roast-friendly harvests year-round.

## Origin and natural habitat

Rosemary grows wild on dry, lime-rich Mediterranean coastlines. The name comes from Latin ros marinus — "dew of the sea" — after the droplets that gather in its flowers.

## Care

### Light

As much sun as possible. Indoors: a south-facing window. Outdoors: a warm, sheltered spot. Swedish winter: keep cool and bright, at 5–15 °C.

### Watering

Let the soil dry between waterings — rosemary prefers drought to damp. Water sparingly in winter.

### Soil and feeding

Sandy, chalky, free-draining soil. Feed sparingly. Prune after flowering for density; dry sprigs whole or ground.

## Common problems

**Brown branches** usually come from overwatering or winter damage. **Powdery leaves** mean mildew in stagnant air. **Leggy growth** signals low light.

## Good to know

Rosemary can live for decades and grows into a small bush with age. It has long been associated with memory — "rosemary for remembrance". Safe for cats and dogs.
