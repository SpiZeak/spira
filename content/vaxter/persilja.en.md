Parsley is the kitchen garden's staple: easy, nutritious and always useful. Curly parsley is the prettiest; flat-leaf has the most flavour.

## Origin and natural habitat

Parsley originates from the Mediterranean and has been grown for over 2,000 years — from Greek cooking to Victorian tables. In the wild it grows on stony ground in humid climates.

## Care

### Light

Sunny to partially shaded. Indoors: a south- or east-facing window. Outdoors: sow in June–July for a winter harvest under fleece.

### Watering

Keep the soil evenly moist — parsley never enjoys drought. Water when the surface dries, preferably in the morning.

### Soil and feeding

Rich, free-draining soil. Sow directly or buy plants; feed lightly through summer. Harvest outer leaves and the plant keeps producing from the centre.

## Common problems

**Yellow leaves** mean nutrient deficiency or drought. **Larvae eating leaves** are parsley worm/caterpillars: pick them off. **Slow germination** is normal — parsley seeds can take three weeks; sow only one centimetre deep.

## Good to know

Parsley is rich in vitamin C and iron — in the Middle Ages it was chewed against bad breath. Safe for cats and dogs.
