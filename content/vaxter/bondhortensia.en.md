The smooth hydrangea (_Hydrangea arborescens_) is North American in origin and the only hydrangea species native to the United States. Its hardy nature has made it a safe choice in Scandinavian gardens — it tolerates cold down to –30 °C in the right position. The cultivar 'Annabelle' is one of the most widely known garden plants, with its iconic white snowballs that can reach 30 cm in diameter.

## Popular Cultivars

**'Annabelle'** — the classic, well-known cultivar with enormous white flower balls. Blooms July–September. One of the best-selling garden shrubs in Scandinavia.

**'Incrediball'** — an enhanced 'Annabelle' with stronger stems that don't bend under the weight of the flower heads. Handles rain and wind better.

**'Invincibelle Spirit'** — a newer cultivar with rose-pink flower clusters, developed as a hardy alternative to bigleaf hydrangea. Blooms long, from July to frost.

**'Hayes Starburst'** — a double-flowered cultivar with star-shaped petals. Rarer but very beautiful.

## Care

### Light

Smooth hydrangea is adaptable — it blooms in everything from full sun to deep shade, but thrives best in partial shade. In full sun it needs generous watering and may need protection from hot midday sun.

### Watering

Despite its hardiness, _H. arborescens_ is moisture-hungry. Water generously and regularly, especially during dry summers. Mulch generously around the base to retain moisture and keep the roots cool.

### Feeding

Fertilise with a balanced flowering fertiliser in May and again in June. Choose a fertiliser with a lower nitrogen ratio to avoid excessive leaf growth at the expense of flowering.

### Pruning

_H. arborescens_ blooms on the current season's growth and can be pruned hard every spring. Cut all stems down to 20–30 cm above ground in April for a new, compact habit and large flower heads. If you skip hard pruning, a bushier crown forms with more but smaller flower clusters — both methods work.

### Overwintering

No overwintering needed in zones 1–4. In zone 5, a layer of leaves or compost around the base is recommended. The dried brown flower heads can usefully be left on through winter — they provide winter interest and protect the buds.

## Planting

Plant in humus-rich, well-draining soil. Add plenty of compost at planting time. _H. arborescens_ establishes quickly and tolerates transplanting better than most shrubs. It works well as a deciduous hedge, in borders, in groups, or as a specimen plant.

## Common Problems

**Drooping flower stems** are the most common complaint about 'Annabelle' — the heavy flower clusters bend the stems in rain. Solve this with the cultivar 'Incrediball', which has stronger stems, or stake with canes and string.

**Sparse flowering** usually results from too much shade or insufficient watering. Move to a brighter position with more sun.
